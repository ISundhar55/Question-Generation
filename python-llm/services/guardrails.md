# ASSESSMENT GENERATION GUARDRAILS
Apply these guardrails to every generated assessment item.

1. Source Quality & Grounding
* Use trusted, relevant, curriculum-aligned sources.
* Every item must be supported by the provided or approved source content.
* Do not use unreliable sources or introduce unsupported facts.
* Context-clue evidence options must use verbatim text from the stimulus, not outside definitions.

2. Alignment, Accuracy & Defensibility
* Align each item directly to the provided content standard, learning objective, and grade level.
* Direct construct measurement: ensure the item measures the intended skill directly and does not rely on unrelated skills (e.g. test reading comprehension, not science recall, on ELA standards).
* One and only one correct answer must exist, and it must be clearly defensible, unambiguous, and factually accurate.
* Item Independence (Zero Cross-Item Cluing): each item must be completely independent and answerable without information or clues from another item.

3. Misconception-Driven Distractors & Answer Validation
* Every distractor must be definitively incorrect, while remaining plausible and relevant.
* Base distractors on common student misconceptions, common procedural errors, overgeneralizations, or partial understandings associated with the target skill.
* Avoid obviously incorrect, humorous, implausible, or throwaway distractors.
* Never include accidentally correct or partially correct distractors (fully validate all calculations, comparative statements, and relationships before finalizing).
* Acceptable alternative answers (for constructed response) must be true grammatical or linguistic synonyms, never scientifically distinct terms (e.g. boiling vs. evaporation).

4. Option Symmetry & Length Neutrality
* Ensure answer options are parallel in grammatical structure (e.g., all full sentences, all verb phrases, or all noun phrases).
* Options must be similar in length, complexity, and level of detail.
* Prevent Length Bias: No correct answer should be noticeably longer, more specific, or more qualified than the distractors.
* Distribute correct answers across available letters (A, B, C, D, etc.) without favoring specific letters.

5. Grade-Appropriateness & Low Linguistic Load
* Calibrate vocabulary, syntax, context, and cognitive demand strictly to the stated grade level.
* Keep linguistic load low: avoid unnecessarily complex sentence structures, unfamiliar contexts, or adult-centered scenarios so reading burden does not exceed what is required by the construct.
* Ensure each item is clear and self-contained.

6. Difficulty & Depth of Knowledge (DOK) Calibration
* Calibrate the item to the requested difficulty level:
  - Easy: Targets foundational knowledge or straightforward application with minimal distractor complexity.
  - Moderate: Requires application of concepts and discrimination among plausible distractors.
  - Challenging: Requires deeper reasoning, analysis, integration of information, or sophisticated distractors based on common misconceptions.
* Match Depth of Knowledge (DOK) level when specified:
  - DOK 1: Recall, recognition, basic computation, or identifying information.
  - DOK 2: Application, categorization, comparison, explanation, or use of a concept.
  - DOK 3: Reasoning, justification, analysis, strategic thinking, or use of evidence.
  - DOK 4: Extended thinking, synthesis, investigation, or complex multi-step reasoning over time.
* Do not label an item as a higher DOK level unless the student task truly requires that level of thinking.

7. Bias, Sensitivity, and Fairness (Universal Equity)
* Avoid stereotypes, assumptions, or deficit-based language related to race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, socioeconomic status, family structure, body type, language background, immigration status, or geographic region.
* Avoid contexts that may be upsetting, traumatic, shame-based, or unnecessarily sensitive unless essential to the standard and handled neutrally.
* Avoid content involving violence, death, serious illness, abuse, disasters, substance use, weapons, political persuasion, religious advocacy, or other sensitive topics unless explicitly requested for an appropriate academic purpose.
* Do not require students to have specialized cultural, regional, religious, financial, or extracurricular knowledge to answer correctly.
* Use authentic, broadly accessible contexts.

8. Key and Distractor Rationales
* Provide a clear, educational rationale synchronized with the answer key.
* Key Rationale: Explain why the correct answer is right based on the concept or text.
* Distractor Rationales: For each distractor, explain the likely misconception, procedural error, or misunderstanding it reflects.
* Tag each option, blank, pair, step, or row breakdown explicitly with `(Correct)` or `(Incorrect)`.
* Keep each bullet strictly limited to 1–2 final, student-facing explanation sentences.

9. Visual Validation (When Visuals Are Requested)
* When visuals are requested, ensure diagrams and charts directly illustrate the intended concept and are mathematically, conceptually, and scientifically accurate.
* Strict visual dependency: the item cannot be answered without analyzing the visual.
* The visual must explicitly contain all referenced measurements, labels, points, or values.
* The answer and distractors must be verifiable from the question and visual without ambiguity.
