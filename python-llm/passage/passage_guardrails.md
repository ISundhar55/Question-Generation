# PASSAGE AUTHORING GUARDRAILS

Apply these universal standards to every generated assessment reading stimulus / passage across all subjects, grades, and genres.

1. Originality & Copyright Protection
* All passages must be 100% original prose synthesized specifically for educational assessment.
* Never copy, scrape, or adapt existing copyrighted texts, standardized test items, or published books.
* Craft fresh, engaging material tailored for student reading.

2. Curriculum & Topic Grounding
* Ground the passage firmly in the specified content area, grade level, and requested topic.
* Present authentic, factually accurate, and well-researched subject matter.
* Avoid extraneous or confusing tangents that distract from the core educational focus.

3. Downstream Question Readiness & Context Clues
* The passage serves as a stimulus for student evaluation. It must provide rich, verifiable textual evidence suitable for varied question formats (e.g. Multiple Choice, Select Text, Vocabulary-in-Context, Constructed Response).
* Include clear organizational structures such as cause-and-effect, chronological sequence, comparison-contrast, or problem-solution.
* Authentic Context Clues: When supporting vocabulary or word-meaning assessment targets, embed clues naturally through surrounding sentences (e.g. contrasting ideas, cause-and-effect relationships, illustrative consequences, or descriptive elaboration).
* ZERO DEFINITION SPOILERS (STRICT): NEVER insert explicit dictionary or appositive formulas such as `", meaning [definition]"`, `"which means [definition]"`, `"that is to say, [definition]"`, or parenthetical glosses like `"(i.e., [definition])"`. Students must actively deduce and infer meanings from context; passages must never give away the answer directly.

4. Grade-Appropriate Readability & Prose Quality
* Calibrate sentence length, vocabulary tier (Tier 2 academic vs. Tier 3 domain-specific), and grammatical complexity strictly to the target grade level.
* Use natural, polished, authentic prose. Avoid awkward redundancies or tautologies (e.g. avoid phrases like "young babies", "past history", "completely finished").
* Ensure the text is fully self-contained: all ideas and vocabulary must be decipherable from within the four corners of the text without requiring outside knowledge or dictionary lookup unless specified by curriculum standards.

5. Structure & Flow
* Provide an engaging, relevant title.
* Write full, cohesive paragraphs with smooth transitions; avoid bulleted lists, disjointed outlines, or dry encyclopedic summaries.
* Ensure a clear opening context, substantial body development with concrete details, and a natural conclusion.

6. Assessment Scope & Boundaries Compliance
* Strictly abide by all teacher-specified Assessment Targets, Boundaries, and Limitations.
* Never introduce concepts, advanced mechanisms, or topics explicitly excluded in the assessment boundaries.
* Align cognitive depth with the requested complexity level (e.g. Bloom's Taxonomy, Webb's DOK).
